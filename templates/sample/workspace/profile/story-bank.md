# Story Bank (STAR)

> SAMPLE: made-up stories for a made-up person, to show how the story bank feeds interview prep and flashcards.
> Interview answers come from here. Each story is **S**ituation, **T**ask, **A**ction, **R**esult, told in about 2 minutes.
> Tag each story with the questions it can answer.

## 1. Launching Northwind Insights (Northwind Software)
Answers: leading a launch, cross-functional work, results, "tell me about a launch you're proud of"
- **S:** Northwind was adding an analytics add-on and sales had no clear story for it.
- **T:** Own positioning and the launch plan.
- **A:** I ran 25 customer interviews, wrote the positioning, and trained the sales team before launch. The hard part was getting product to drop two features from the launch message that customers didn't care about.
- **R:** 1,200 paying accounts in the first two quarters, and the company's "Launch of the Year" award.
- Likely follow-ups an interviewer in this field will ask: How did you pick the launch metric? What would you cut if the budget were half?

## 2. Battlecards that sales used (Northwind Software)
Answers: influencing without authority, data-driven decisions, working with sales
- **S:** Account executives were losing deals to one competitor, and the old battlecards sat unread.
- **T:** Give them something they would actually use in calls.
- **A:** I built a battlecard program with the 40 account executives and updated it every quarter from lost-deal notes.
- **R:** Win rate against that competitor rose from 31% to 38%.
- Likely follow-ups: How did you get reps to send lost-deal notes? How did you keep it current?

## 3. Webinar program growth (Globex Analytics)
Answers: initiative, growth, experimenting
- **S:** Globex's webinars drew about 300 registrants a quarter.
- **T:** Make them a real pipeline source.
- **A:** I changed topics to customer-led sessions and promoted them through partners.
- **R:** 2,100 registrants per quarter.

## 4. The pricing page test that almost didn't happen (Globex Analytics)
Answers: failure or mistake, learning, data-driven decisions
- **S:** My first pricing page test ran for two weeks before I noticed the tracking only counted one of the two demo forms.
- **T:** Save the test and win back the team's trust in the numbers.
- **A:** I told my manager the same day, fixed the tracking with the web team, and restarted the test with a written plan everyone signed off on first.
- **R:** The rerun lifted demo requests by 18%, and the written test plan became the team's template.
- What I'd do differently: check every conversion event before launch, not after.

## 5. Two teams, one message (Northwind Software)
Answers: conflict, disagreeing with a decision, influencing
- **S:** Product wanted to lead the Insights launch with a feature list; sales wanted a cost-savings story.
- **T:** Land one message both teams would use.
- **A:** I brought quotes from the customer interviews to a short working session and we tested both versions in two email campaigns.
- **R:** The cost-savings version won on replies by a wide margin, product agreed, and we kept a feature appendix for technical buyers.
