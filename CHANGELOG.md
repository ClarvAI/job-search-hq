# Changelog

What changed in each version of Job Search HQ, newest first. `/update` shows you the entries newer than the version you have. Versions follow `MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH`: a patch fixes things, a minor version adds things, a major version changes how something you rely on works.

## 1.1.0 (2026-10-22)

**After updating from 1.0.0, close and reopen this folder in Claude** so the new panels (Insights, Routines, Celebration) and the changed ones load.

- **Posting watch (a new routine):** once a week it checks whether the saved jobs you haven't applied to yet are still open, and `/today` lists the ones that look closed, with an offer to mark them skipped. It only reads; you decide. You can also just ask "are my saved jobs still open?".
- **Routines that stop running get noticed:** when a routine hasn't run for longer than its schedule allows (the app was closed, the computer slept, the folder moved, or a run is waiting for a permission answer), the next time you open the app it tells you, and `/health` lists the usual causes. Pausing or deleting one on purpose is remembered, so it isn't flagged.
- **Easier to find:** setup and `/today` each mention automatic checks once.
- **New panel, `/insights`:** how your search is going, read-only. It shows where applications stall, applications per week, where replies come from, whether jobs you scored as a strong fit really hear back more often, and why you passed on leads. It works in the chat too ("how is my search going?").
- **New panel, `/routines-board`:** each automatic check, when it last ran and what it found, and a note above the prompt if one stopped running. Press **I paused it** or **I deleted it** after doing that in the app, so it isn't flagged.
- **Pipeline:** a saved job whose posting looks closed says so, with a one-press **Skip it: posting closed**.
- **You got the job:** the first time a job becomes accepted, a congratulations opens by itself (Claude), with your search in numbers and a button to wrap up. `/share-lessons` (Claude and Codex) then asks five quick questions and drafts anonymous notes about what worked, checked for names, companies, contact details, pay and dates, for you to post on the project's GitHub page if you want. The assistant never posts anything itself, and the safety check now blocks it from posting on GitHub.
- **A contingent offer** (background check, references, a degree still to come) no longer closes your search at once: other final-round processes stay open until it clears.
- **Smarter search:** `/insights` compares jobs with a contact against those without, and applying within 3 days against later. Pasting six or more job links offers a quick first pass into the Job Inbox before the full workup.
- **Lighter on small plans:** a scheduled check with nothing to do (setup not finished, your search paused or closed, this week's review already done) now stops after a quick script instead of reading everything; the suggested schedules are cheaper (job scans three times a week, follow-up drafts twice); and if you're on a $20 plan, say so and the assistant saves effort on lower-priority jobs while keeping every fact check.
- **From your phone:** setup can show you how to check in from the Claude or ChatGPT phone app while your computer does the work.
- **Sturdier panels:** a file that can't be read (a cloud file that isn't downloaded, a sync lock) now shows a clear message with Refresh and Close instead of loading forever; the Job Inbox holds back leads with a missing or repeated id instead of risking a change to the wrong one; the congratulations pop-up shows only when the change really saved; empty boards no longer show next to a "damaged file" message; the Interview Room has a Close button before an interview starts; and error messages start with "Not done:" in every panel.
- **PDF checks catch more:** besides name, contact details, headings and dates, the finished resume's text is checked for unreadable characters (an icon or a font with no text behind it, which application systems read as garbage), and the layout for a last page with only a few lines or a heading left alone at the bottom of a page.
- **Keyword coverage you can act on:** each tailored resume's notes show, for every key requirement, whether you meet it and whether it's on the page in the posting's words, in your own words, left off for space, or a real gap. Gaps are never filled in for you.
- **Blocked isn't closed:** a job site that refuses to load (an error page or a block) no longer counts as proof the job is gone. A posting is marked closed only when it says so, redirects away, or drops out of the employer's job feed; otherwise you're asked to check it.
- **Your fit scores learn from replies:** once about 10 applications have a result, the weekly review compares fit scores with who actually replied and says whether to aim wider or tighter; `/evaluate-job` uses that on borderline jobs.
- **Skill gaps:** the weekly review lists the three skills your jobs most often ask for and you don't show yet, each with one way to close or bridge it, and builds a sourced learning plan if you ask ("what should I learn?").
- **Your public work:** `/resume-review` can read your GitHub, portfolio, publications or talks and suggest real projects your resume leaves out. Each one is added only after you confirm it's yours and correct.

## 1.0.0 (2026-10-16)

First public release.

- Job-search workspace for Claude Code and Codex, on Mac and Windows: setup interview, daily plan, job finding and evaluation, tailored resumes and cover letters, outreach and follow-ups, interview prep and spoken mock interviews, offers, weekly reviews.
- Panels in Claude Code: Outbox, Pipeline, Contacts, Job Inbox, Apply Kit, Interview Room, due alerts.
- Safety checks: the assistant never sends email, never invites people to calendar events, never uses the browser on Gmail, Outlook or LinkedIn, and never pushes this folder to a public GitHub repository.
- Your files and the app's files are kept apart (`DATA_CONTRACT.md`): your files are set up from blank copies on the first run, are never part of the download, and are never changed by an update.
- `/update` installs a new version from a release zip, keeps any app file you edited as `<file>.local`, backs up what it replaces, and can roll back.
- `/my-data` backs up, wraps up, or resets your data.
- `/health` checks that everything is set up and working, changes nothing, and names the one thing to fix first.
- Layoff first-week plan (`/laid-off`), unemployment work-search log (`/work-search-log`), and an optional visa unemployment clock in `/today` and `/evaluate-job`.
- **Your dashboard on your phone, two ways:** in Claude, besides your themed page, you can publish it as a private Claude Dashboard where every number shows where it came from and a warning appears when it's more than three days old. Either way, the published copy now shows only your job list and counts: no contacts' names, notes, pay or activity log.
- **Try it first:** ask for the sample workspace and you get a separate practice copy filled with a made-up job search, next to your folder and never inside it, so you can try the boards and pages before entering anything.
- **Your language:** set `language:` and the assistant talks with you in it, writes dates and money your way, and shows a translated phrase beside each typed confirm phrase. Mock interviews can be held in another language, read aloud with a computer voice for that language.
- **More kinds of searches:** guides for searching while employed, hourly and shift work (availability, hours and shifts in your preferences, filled in by the Apply Kit), freelance and contract work, and academic jobs, plus working with recruiters and search firms.
- **More inboxes:** `/sync-inbox` reads a Microsoft 365 work or school mailbox, or emails you paste or save, as well as Gmail.
- **Automatic checks (opt-in):** say "set up automatic checks" for routines that run on your own computer in the Claude or ChatGPT desktop app: a morning job scan, an inbox check, follow-up drafts, a weekly review, or a benefits certification reminder. They only read, draft and track (never send, apply or change your profile), ask no questions, and the next time you open the app it tells you what they found.
- **Attachments:** a draft can name the job's checked PDF (`attach:`), and the Outbox panel attaches it when it makes the Gmail draft. Sending still never carries attachments.
- **Sound only when you ask:** the Interview Room's read-aloud and timer beeps are off until you say yes or press **Turn on voice** / **Turn on sounds**, and it remembers your choice.
- **Two writers, no lost changes:** a panel that finds its file changed since it opened (the other app, Excel, or another panel) refuses to save and shows you the latest version.
- **More looks:** three new presets (editorial, calm, bold); a timeline dashboard, printable cheat cards for flashcards, a one-column portfolio, and a portrait designed resume (a photo only if you ask for one). New templates for an academic CV, a US federal resume (USAJOBS, two pages at most) and a Europass-style CV.
- **Pages work offline:** the fonts and libraries now come with the app, so your pages on your computer load nothing from the web. Resume PDFs embed proper fonts and have a clickable outline.
- **Other connected apps help when you have them:** if you've connected company data (such as PitchBook or Crunchbase), a file store, meeting notes, a contacts app or a presentation maker to Claude or Codex, the assistant uses it, read only, to check a company's funding and headcount before you apply, find recently funded employers, add startup funding history to an offer review (never as what your options are worth), bring in your resume from Google Drive, or debrief from your own interview notes (only with your OK). It notes where each fact came from and when, keeps paid database data out of anything shared, and warns you once before using an employer's account. Nothing new to install.
- **Alumni:** `/network` and setup help you find people from your school, bootcamp or former employer at a company, and the employers where many of them work, through steps you do yourself on LinkedIn, your school's alumni directory and public pages (never automated). Counts are saved with the date on your target employers and in each job's fit notes, and a "warm path" moves a job up in `/find-jobs`.
- New guides: getting started, platforms, and where your information goes (`docs/privacy.md`).

Fixed and tightened before release, after end-to-end walkthroughs:

- **Dates:** every reader (panels, startup summary, status line, chat) reads dates the same way. `YYYY-MM-DD` and day-first dotted dates (`9.10.2026`) are always understood; a slash date is read from the date itself, the rest of its row, or the new `date_order:` setting. Anything still unclear shows as "date unclear", never triggers an alert, and is never rewritten. Where you live no longer decides how a date is read.
- **Spreadsheets:** a stray quote mark in a cell no longer swallows the rows after it, and a file that was cut off mid-quote or saved by Excel with semicolons gets a clear warning instead of being saved over. Blank CSV files start with a byte-order mark so Excel on Windows shows accents correctly, and accented letters are always kept. Instructions for Excel in Europe (open with Data → From Text/CSV, or use Google Sheets).
- **Your look is your data:** themed resume and cover-letter templates now live in `profile/templates/`, so the app's own templates stay untouched, `git pull` no longer conflicts, and an update never re-themes your pages. When an update changes a template, your copy is rebuilt in your look and the old one kept as `<name>.previous.html`. Theme previews go to `pages/.preview/`, where the built-in browser can open them.
- **Pages:** the data inside each page is escaped correctly, so text containing `</script>` can't break out of it, and a check guards against it.
- **Safety:** commands that would copy over, move, delete or rewrite your master resume or preferences now ask first; the Outbox panel asks you to confirm send mode once in the panel before it sends; assisted mode is confirmed in the chat each session. The safety check now follows git aliases, `-c` settings and address rewrites, blocks public gists and commands that hide what they run, decodes redirect links, and covers more calendar, meeting and mail-like connectors. The docs say plainly what it still doesn't cover (scripts saved in files, other hosting sites, sites that redirect on their own, time-outs), and the optional pre-push safety net is offered by setup and `/health`.
- **Setup and preferences:** setup records which sections are done in its own checklist file (`profile/setup-progress.md`, which it updates without asking) and picks up where it stopped, so `profile/preferences.md` is only edited when the user's answers change (a `setup_progress:` line left in preferences by an earlier build is still read and moved over). Work authorization is stored as one exact line per country (`- Work authorization (US): Yes`, `- Needs sponsorship (US): No`) that the Apply Kit reads. New optional lines for benefits (`benefits_claim_start:`, `benefits_cert_covers:` for two-week certifications) and the visa clock (`visa_days_used_as_of:`, with one clear way to count days left). Salary floors take a currency, per country. Field-pack files say which field they were built for, so a change of field gets them rebuilt.
- **Fewer prompts:** setup saves your master resume once and your preferences at most twice (each change still shown to you first), so a new setup brings up about three permission boxes instead of six; and in Claude Code, a session set to ask before every step tells you once, on your first message, how to switch to auto mode.
- **Tracking:** a take-home and a booked interview no longer overwrite each other (the second goes in notes as `Also: ...`); thank-you notes and updates to people who already helped you don't count as unanswered follow-ups; new `Schedule interview` next step.
- **Scams:** new red flag for "app tester" jobs that have you open bank accounts or do video identity checks (common in Germany), and clearer advice on when an ID check before an offer is legitimate.
- **Executive search:** advice on working with recruiters and headhunters for experienced hires.
- **Routines:** pausing your search now pauses the automatic checks too (they skip until your resume date, and pausing or accepting a job reminds you to pause or delete them), while a benefits certification reminder keeps running until your new job starts. An inbox check that couldn't finish no longer moves the window forward, and the follow-up routine drafts the second status check after an interview.
- **Better answers:** the recruiter-screen pay script checks the posted range against your minimum before saying it works; academic cover letters keep their length through the voice check; hourly jobs get a 4-business-day follow-up; referral asks come sooner for postings that review on a rolling basis.
