# Interview Question Bank

Used by `/interview-prep` (pick the likely ones for a job) and `/mock-interview` (draw realistic questions, mixing common and role-specific). Each behavioral question names the competency interviewers score; match it to a story in `profile/story-bank.md`. Role-specific technical questions for the user's target roles are in `knowledge/field/role-questions.md` (generated by `/start`); technical answers live in `knowledge/field/field-primer.md`.

## 1. Openers every interview has

| Question | What they want | Where the answer comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Walk me through your resume. / Tell me about yourself. | 60–90 seconds, one thread, ends on why this role | The user's master resume; a thread the user agrees with |
| Why this field? | A real reason, not an abstraction | The user's own words in the story bank ("Why this field?") |
| Why this company? / Why this role? | One true, specific thing about them + one thing the user did that matches | `fit.md` and `company-brief.md` |
| What do you know about our products / services? | Did you prepare | `company-brief.md`, 3 bullets |

## 2. Behavioral, by competency

Score with the behavioral rubric in `/mock-interview` (STAR, "I" not "we", a number, what was learned). Real interviewers probe: be ready for "What was your role?", "How did it turn out?", "What would you do differently?"

| Competency | Questions |
|---|---|
| Problem solving / root cause | Tell me about a problem you solved using data. Walk me through how you found the cause of a problem. |
| Process improvement / cost | A time you improved a process or saved time or money. |
| Initiative / ownership | A time you went beyond your assigned work. Something you started on your own. |
| Learning fast | A time you had to learn a new system or skill quickly. |
| Influence without authority | A time you convinced someone senior. A time you sold an idea. |
| Teamwork / conflict | A disagreement with a teammate or manager and how it ended. |
| Leadership | A time you led a team or a project. |
| Failure / mistake | Tell me about a failure. A mistake you made and what you changed. (Must be real.) |
| Attention to detail | A time your attention to detail caught a problem. |
| Prioritization / pressure | Juggling deadlines; a time you were overwhelmed. |
| Customer / stakeholder focus | A time you had to understand what a user or customer needed. |
| Ethics / integrity | A time you were asked to do something you weren't comfortable with. |
| Adaptability | A time priorities changed suddenly. |
| Handling ambiguity | A time you had to act without all the information. |
| Receiving feedback | A time you got critical feedback and what you changed. |
| Data-driven decision | A time data changed your mind. |
| Customer / stakeholder conflict | A time two people you served wanted different things. |

## 3. Judgment and ethics

- You find an error in work you already signed off on. What do you do?
- You see a coworker skip a required step. What do you do?
- Your result looks bad right before a deadline. What do you do?
- A customer or client asks for something that breaks a rule. What do you do?
- What would you do if you were given a task you didn't know how to do and your manager was unavailable?

What good looks like almost everywhere: don't fix it quietly, document it, tell the right person, follow the process, and learn from it. Fields with specific rules (safety, compliance, privacy, client confidentiality) have their own version in `knowledge/field/field-primer.md` section 5.

## 5. Stage-specific questions

**Students and new grads**
- Rotational or graduate programs: which rotations interest you, and why? Where do you see yourself after the program?
- Relocation: are you open to any location? (Answer honestly from `profile/preferences.md`.)
- Why should we hire you over someone with more experience? (What the user has actually done, concretely.)

**Career changers**
- Why the change, and why now? (Short, forward-looking, no apology.)
- What carries over from your previous field? (Two concrete transferable skills with proof.)
- How have you prepared? (Courses, projects, volunteering: only what's real.)
- Why should we take a chance on someone without direct experience? (Proof of learning speed plus one transferable result.)

**Returning after a gap**
- Tell me about the gap. (One or two plain sentences, then pivot to readiness.)
- What did you do to stay current? (Only what's real; health details are never required.)

**After a layoff**
- Why were you let go? (One factual sentence, one result you're proud of, then forward. See `.claude/skills/interview-prep/references/formats-and-hard-questions.md`.)

**Senior and management roles**
- Tell me about a team you built or turned around.
- How do you decide what not to do?
- A time you had to deliver bad news upward.

**Any stage**
- One-way video screens (HireVue-style): usually 3–6 questions, about 30 seconds to think, 1–3 minutes to answer. Practice with `/mock-interview <job> one-way`.
- AI interviewer (bot by phone, video or chat): answer each question fully in one turn, with an example and a number. Practice with `/mock-interview <job> ai-screen`.
- Recruiter phone screen: must-haves, work authorization, location, start date, pay. See `/interview-prep`.
- Presentation round: "Present a project in 10–15 minutes." See the presentation section in `/interview-prep`.
- Case or exercise rounds (consulting, product, analytics, design, sales role-plays): see `knowledge/field/field-primer.md` for the format in this field.

## 6. Curveballs

- What's your greatest weakness? (A real, non-fatal one plus what you're doing about it.)
- What salary are you looking for? (See the salary guidance in `/interview-prep`; ask for their range first.)
- Do you have other interviews or offers? (Honest, brief, positive.)
- Why are you leaving your current job? (Toward something, never trashing anyone.)
- What would your last manager say you need to work on? (Real and specific.)
- Why should we hire you? (Two requirements from the posting, each with one real proof.)
- Are you legally authorized to work here / will you need sponsorship? (Allowed and common; answer plainly.)
- Questions employers shouldn't ask (age, family plans, religion, national origin, disability): how to respond is in the interview-prep references.
- Is there anything you'd like us to know that we didn't ask?

## 7. Questions to ask them (pick 4–6 for the person you're talking to)

(Match the person: recruiter = process, timeline, range; hiring manager = success and challenges; peers = a typical week.)

- What does the first 90 days look like for someone new in this role?
- How will you measure the success of the person in this role?
- What are the biggest challenges someone in this role will face?
- What separates people who do really well here in their first year?
- What's the biggest challenge the team is working on right now?
- How does the team decide which projects to take on?
- How is training structured? (early career)
- What do you enjoy most about working here?
- **Always last:** What are the next steps and the timeline?
