# Searching while you have a job

Shared background for `find-jobs`, `linkedin`, `network`, `interview-prep`, `apply` and `offer` when the user still has a job (the master resume shows a current role, or they say so). Load it only then. Checked 2026-10-13; sources at the end. Laws and settings change: check the official page before quoting a rule.

Ask once, early: "Does your current employer know you're looking?" If not, follow the discretion rules below without making a fuss about it. Save the answer as a dated line under "Notes from conversations" in `profile/preferences.md` (with the user's yes, like any change there).

## 1. Discretion

- **Their own devices and accounts only.** Search, apply and take calls on a personal phone, computer and email address, never the employer's laptop, email, chat or Wi-Fi. Employers can usually see what happens on their own equipment and accounts (career and legal advice columns agree; in the UK the regulator's guidance on monitoring workers explains when it's allowed). This folder should live on a personal computer for the same reason.
- **LinkedIn:** Open to Work set to **Recruiters only**, never the public green frame. LinkedIn tries to hide it from recruiters at the user's current company but says it can't guarantee that (`.claude/skills/linkedin/references/linkedin-facts.md`). Before editing the headline or experience, turn off **Share profile updates** (Me → Settings & Privacy → Visibility → Visibility of your LinkedIn activity), so a new headline or job edit isn't announced to their network. Some edits never send a notice, and anyone viewing the profile sees changes as soon as they're saved. Don't change the headline to "Looking for new opportunities".
- **People:** don't ask colleagues at the current employer for introductions or references; former colleagues, former managers and people outside the company are the safe route. Anyone can talk, so tell only people the user trusts.
- **Public posts:** no "I'm looking" posts, no resume on public job boards with the current employer's name visible (many boards let them hide it or make the resume private; tell them to check the board's privacy setting).
- **Applications:** some forms ask "May we contact your current employer?" The honest answer "Not yet; after an offer" is normal and widely accepted.
- **Don't badmouth the current employer**, in interviews, messages or anywhere.

## 2. Interviews around a job

- Ask for early-morning, lunchtime or end-of-day slots, or video calls, and batch rounds on the same day where possible. Recruiters are used to this; one sentence does it ("I'm currently employed, so early morning or after 4 works best").
- Use personal or vacation time for long onsite days rather than inventing reasons. Never take interview calls on a work device or in the office.
- The user never has to say why they're leaving in detail: a short, forward-looking reason ("I want to do more X, which my role doesn't offer") is enough (`.claude/skills/interview-prep/references/formats-and-hard-questions.md`).

## 3. References when the boss doesn't know

- Use former managers, a trusted former colleague, a client, or a senior colleague who has left. Tell the employer plainly: "My current manager doesn't know I'm looking; I'm happy for you to contact them once we've agreed an offer." Asking for an offer to be conditional on a satisfactory current-employer reference is common and widely accepted.
- Line references up in `profile/references.md` only with their permission.

## 4. Notice period and the start date

- **The user's contract decides** how much notice they owe. Read it before naming a start date.
- **US:** most jobs are at-will (either side can end them at any time, unless a contract or law says otherwise); two weeks' notice is the custom, not a legal rule. A contract can say more.
- **UK:** at least one week's notice after a month in the job, or more if the contract says so; many contracts say one to three months. Garden leave (staying employed and paid but not working during the notice) can be required only if the contract allows it; otherwise it needs the user's agreement.
- **Elsewhere:** notice set by law or contract is often one to three months (Germany, the Netherlands, France and others); look up the country's rule and read the contract.
- The earliest start date in `profile/preferences.md` is "about <notice> after accepting an offer" (`start` asks for the notice period). Use it in `application-answers.md` and the recruiter screen.
- **Resign only after a written, unconditional offer** (background check and references cleared, start date agreed). Then resign in writing, briefly and politely, and offer a handover.

## 5. Counteroffers

If the current employer counters after the user resigns, use `/offer` (its reference, section 4): ask "Would I have stayed if they'd offered this a month ago?" before comparing numbers, and never use a counteroffer to bargain with the new employer after accepting.

## 6. If the user is found out or let go

Stay calm and honest; don't deny it if asked directly, and don't elaborate. If the job ends, `/laid-off` covers the first week.
