## Negotiation scripts (one ask, one reason, warm)

**When the offer first comes (often by phone): don't accept or negotiate on the spot:**
> Thank you, I'm really glad to hear it. Could you send the details in writing? I'd like to look everything over, and I'll get back to you by [date]. When do you need an answer?

**If they push for a number ("What would it take?"):**
> I want to make this work. Let me look at the full package in writing and come back to you with one clear answer by [date].

**Signing bonus or relocation (useful when base is fixed):**
> Thank you again for the offer; I'm excited about the team. Moving to [city] will cost about $[X] up front. Is there any flexibility on the signing bonus or relocation to help with that?

**Base salary (market data):**
> Thank you again for the offer. Based on what I'm seeing for similar roles in [city], I was expecting something closer to $[X]. Is there flexibility on base?

(Use $[X] only from the sourced market range in `offer.md`; have the source ready in case they ask.)

**Competing offer (only if real; never invent one):**
> I have another offer with a decision date of [date]. [Company] is my first choice. Is there any flexibility on [base or sign-on] that would help me say yes this week?

**Start date:**
> Would a start date of [date] work? I'd like to [wrap up my current commitments / finish my program] and move without rushing.

**Deadline extension:**
> I'm very interested in this role because [one specific thing about the team, product or program] and want to make a careful decision. I'm expecting to hear from one other company by [date]. Would it be possible to extend my decision deadline to [date]?

**Very short deadline (24–72 hours):**
> Thank you. This is a big decision and I take commitments seriously, so I can't responsibly decide in [48 hours]. Could we make it [date, about a week out]? I'll give you a firm answer by then.

(Ask for a modest extension, usually 1–2 weeks. For students and new grads, check whether the school's career center publishes an employer offer-timeline policy, and mention it only if it exists. Fill in the brackets with something real before sending.)

**Closing the ask (makes it easy to say yes; only if the user really would sign):**
> If you're able to get to [$X / that sign-on], I'm ready to accept today.

## Fine print checklist (read before accepting)

- [ ] The offer is in writing, with base, sign-on, relocation, start date, location, and title
- [ ] Signing bonus and relocation repayment (clawback) terms: how much, over how many months
- [ ] Contingencies and what could cause the offer to be withdrawn
- [ ] Benefits start date (day one, or after 30–90 days)
- [ ] For structured or rotational programs: how assignments are made, and what happens at the end
- [ ] Non-compete or non-solicit clauses (flag any)
- [ ] Equity: type (RSUs or options), amount, vesting schedule and cliff, what vests in year one; for options, strike price, shares outstanding, and the exercise window after leaving (see `negotiation-and-fine-print.md`)
- [ ] Bonus: target, and what it actually paid the last year or two (ask)
- [ ] Retirement match: rate, cap, and how long until the match is vested
- [ ] Background check: written consent form only; the user enters ID details on the screening company's site, never by email

## Accept / decline language

**Accept:** "I'm happy to accept the offer for [title], starting [date]. Thank you. Please let me know the next steps for onboarding."

**Decline:** "Thank you so much for the offer and the time your team spent with me. After a lot of thought, I've accepted another position. I hope our paths cross again."
