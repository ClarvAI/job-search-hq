# Negotiation, equity, benefits, and the steps after "yes"

Background for `/offer`. Scripts live in `offer-worksheet.md`; market numbers come from `pay-data.md`. Sources at the end (checked 2026-10). Laws and norms change; look up the current rule before quoting one.

## 1. How negotiation really goes

- **Most people don't ask, and asking usually helps a little.** In Pew's 2023 survey of about 5,000 US workers, under a third asked for more than the first offer. Of those who asked, 28% got what they asked for, 38% got more than offered but less than asked, and 35% got no change. So: ask once, politely, with a reason, and expect a partial yes or a no, not a fight.
- **Rescinded offers for a polite ask are rare** but they happen, mostly at small employers or after ultimatums, repeated asks, or asking for more after agreeing. Hiring-advice columns and Reddit threads agree on that much; the "under 2%" style statistics online have no traceable source. Keep it to one warm, specific ask and the risk is small.
- **The rules that hold up** (Haseeb Qureshi's "Ten Rules," patio11's salary essay, and recruiter guides say the same): get everything in writing; don't accept or reject on the call; give a reason for every ask; negotiate the whole package (base, sign-on, start date, relocation, title, remote days, learning budget); stay warm and make it easy to say yes ("If you can get to $X, I'm ready to sign").
- **Don't name a number first if you can avoid it,** but where a range is posted or the employer insists, a researched range beats stonewalling.

## 2. By career stage (`career_stage` in preferences)

| Stage | What usually moves | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Student / new grad, structured program or cohort | Sign-on, relocation, start date, location or rotation choice | Base often fixed for the class; asking is still fine. Check the school's offer-timeline policy. |
| Student / new grad, regular role | Base a little (often a few thousand), sign-on, start date | A real competing offer or posted ranges for the same title are the strongest reasons. |
| Early career (2–5 yrs) | Base, sign-on, title/level | Level matters more than a few thousand on base: it sets future raises. |
| Mid / senior | Base, level, equity, sign-on to cover lost bonus or unvested equity, remote terms, severance | Ask about refresh grants and the bonus history (what it actually paid). |
| Career changer / returning | Start date, training, title, then base | Lead with what transfers; don't discount yourself in advance. |

## 3. Exploding offers and deadlines

- Career services groups (following NACE principles) treat about two weeks as a reasonable time to decide for students, and call offers that pressure an on-the-spot or 24–48 hour decision "exploding offers." Many schools ask employers to leave fall offers open until a set date; check the user's school.
- Most employers will extend a short deadline if asked directly and politely; some won't. Script in the worksheet. Never invent another offer to get time.

## 4. Counteroffers from a current employer

- If the user resigns and gets a counteroffer: first ask "Would I have stayed if they'd offered this a month ago?" If the reasons for leaving were not about money (manager, growth, the work), money rarely fixes them.
- The popular "80% leave within a year" figure has no traceable study. The best-documented number is a Robert Half (staffing firm) UK survey from 2021: 53% of people offered a counteroffer accepted it, and 74% of those had left within a year. Treat it as a hint, not a law.
- Never use a counteroffer to bargain with the new employer after accepting. That is reneging.

## 5. Equity basics (explain in plain words)

- **RSUs (public companies):** shares granted at a dollar value, delivered as they vest. Typical: 4 years, 1-year cliff (nothing until month 12, then 25%), then monthly or quarterly. Some employers back-load (e.g., 5% / 15% / 40% / 40%), so year-one value can be much lower than "grant ÷ 4." Use the actual schedule from the offer. RSUs are taxed as income when they vest.
- **Refreshers:** later grants that keep annual equity level. Ask whether they're typical and how they're decided; don't count them as guaranteed.
- **Stock options (private startups):** the right to buy shares at the strike price (set by a 409A valuation). Ask: number of options, strike price, total shares outstanding (to get the percentage of the company), latest 409A value, vesting and cliff, and the post-termination exercise window (often 90 days, which can force a big purchase on leaving). Treat startup equity as worth $0 for comparison, plus upside. Taxes on options (ISO vs NSO, AMT) are a question for a tax professional, not for this assistant.
- **First-year equity in the comparison table:** only what actually vests in the first 12 months, at today's price, labeled as an estimate.

## 6. Valuing benefits (estimates, labeled)

- **Retirement match:** match rate × the salary it applies to. "100% up to 6%" on $70,000 = up to $4,200 a year, only if the user contributes 6%. Check how long the match takes to vest.
- **Health insurance:** (employee premium per paycheck × paychecks a year) + a typical year's out-of-pocket (deductible is a fair stand-in). Add any employer HSA deposit as a plus.
- **PTO:** extra days × (base ÷ 260) is a rough cash value.
- **Other:** tuition reimbursement (and its payback clause), commuter or remote stipend, bonus target × the percent it actually paid recently (ask).
- Keep base, first-year cash, and estimated benefits on separate lines; never blend them into one "total" without showing the parts.

## 7. Background checks and contingencies (US)

- Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act, an employer using a screening company must tell the user and get written permission first. If it plans to pull the offer because of the report, it must first send a copy of the report and a summary of rights, and give them a chance to explain or correct errors (commonly about five business days). The user can dispute errors with the screening company.
- Help the user prepare: dates of past jobs and degrees exactly as on the resume (mismatches are the usual problem), and a calm sentence for anything they expect to come up. Some states and cities limit when criminal history can be asked about ("ban the box"); look up the rule for the job's location if it matters.
- Never put ID numbers, SSN or date of birth in this folder; the user enters them on the screening company's site themselves, only after a written offer from a verifiable employer.

## 8. Reneging

Accepting and then backing out burns the relationship, and schools commonly restrict career-services or job-board access for students who do it (some notify the department). If the user is truly considering it, talk it through, check the offer letter for repayment terms, and if they go ahead, tell the employer promptly, by phone first, with an apology and no excuses.

## 9. Offers outside the US

Sections 6 and 7 are US-specific (401(k) match, health premiums, the FCRA). Elsewhere, look up the job country's rules and compare these instead (checked 2026-10-12; confirm on the official page before quoting):
- **Notice period:** the user's current contract sets how much notice they must give, and in the UK and much of Europe it is often one to three months, so it decides the realistic start date. Agree the start date in writing before resigning (UK: "check your employment contract", https://www.gov.uk/handing-in-your-notice).
- **Probation:** common outside the US. In Germany an agreed probation (*Probezeit*) lasts at most six months, and either side can end the job with two weeks' notice during it (§ 622(3) BGB, https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__622.html). Note its length and notice terms in the comparison.
- **Pay structure:** monthly salary × 12, or × 13-14 where the offer states a 13th or 14th month; holiday pay; collective-agreement (tariff) pay grades. Compare annual gross base to annual gross base.
- **Paid leave:** a legal minimum applies (at least four weeks in the EU, under the Working Time Directive 2003/88/EC; 5.6 weeks in the UK, https://www.gov.uk/holiday-entitlement-rights); compare the days above the minimum.
- **Pension:** in the UK, automatic enrolment means at least 8% of qualifying earnings in total, at least 3% from the employer (https://www.gov.uk/workplace-pensions/what-you-your-employer-and-the-government-pay); an employer paying more is worth a line in the table. Health cover is usually not tied to the job; note private health cover only as a perk.
- **Non-compete and garden leave:** rules differ a lot (in Germany a post-employment non-compete needs compensation of at least half the last pay, § 74 HGB, https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/hgb/__74.html). Flag any clause and suggest a local employment lawyer or union before signing; don't judge enforceability.
