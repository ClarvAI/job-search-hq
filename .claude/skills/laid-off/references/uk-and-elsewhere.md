# Redundancy in the UK, and job loss elsewhere

Checked 2026-10-08 on gov.uk and acas.org.uk. Figures change every April and the law is changing under the Employment Rights Act 2025, so re-read the linked page before quoting a number, and give the date you checked. Not legal advice: for anything about the user's own case, point them to Acas (free helpline 0300 123 1100, Monday to Friday 8am to 6pm; https://www.acas.org.uk/contact/get-advice) or a solicitor. These rules are for England, Scotland and Wales; Northern Ireland has its own (nidirect.gov.uk and the Labour Relations Agency).

## UK: what to put in the plan

**1. Benefits: claim now.** Universal Credit (https://www.gov.uk/universal-credit) and, for people with enough National Insurance contributions, New Style Jobseeker's Allowance (https://www.gov.uk/jobseekers-allowance), which is paid for up to about 6 months. Both come with a Claimant Commitment and work search; offer `/work-search-log` once they've claimed. Plan line: claim on the first working day after the job ends (or today).

**2. Statutory redundancy pay** (https://www.gov.uk/redundant-your-rights/redundancy-pay):
- Employees with 2 or more years with the employer are usually entitled to it: half a week's pay for each full year under age 22, one week's pay for each full year from 22 to 40, one and a half weeks' pay for each full year aged 41 or older, counting up to 20 years.
- Weekly pay is capped: for redundancies on or after 6 April 2026, £751 a week, so at most £22,530 (on the page 2026-10-08).
- Calculator: https://www.gov.uk/calculate-your-redundancy-pay. Use it with the user's figures if they want an estimate; say it's an estimate and the contract may give more.
- If it isn't paid, the claim time limit for statutory redundancy pay is 6 months.

**3. Notice** (https://www.gov.uk/redundant-your-rights/notice-periods): at least 1 week if employed 1 month to 2 years; 1 week per full year from 2 to 12 years; 12 weeks after 12 years. The contract may give more. Pay in lieu of notice is allowed if the contract says so. Plan line: the last day of the notice period, and the date notice pay should arrive.

**4. Time off to look for work** (https://www.acas.org.uk/your-rights-during-redundancy/finding-a-job-with-a-new-employer): with 2 or more years' continuous service, reasonable paid time off during notice to look for work or arrange training (the pay for it can be capped at 40% of a week's pay). Use it for interviews; a short note to the manager asking for it is a fine draft.

**5. Settlement agreements** (https://www.acas.org.uk/settlement-agreements):
- A settlement agreement is only legally valid if the user has received advice from a **relevant independent adviser** (a qualified lawyer, a certified trade union official, or a certified advice-centre worker) who is insured and named in the agreement. Employers often contribute to the cost, but don't have to.
- The Acas Code of Practice recommends at least **10 calendar days** to consider it.
- Plan lines: "Find an independent adviser (solicitor or union) to review the agreement" this week; and the agreement's own deadline. The assistant never reviews the terms for fairness.

**6. Tribunal time limits (for the user's awareness only, not to suggest a claim)** (https://www.acas.org.uk/employment-tribunal-time-limits, updated 2026-10-01): for problems on or after 1 October 2026, most claims have a limit of 6 months less one day; for earlier problems, mostly 3 months less one day. Early conciliation through Acas comes first (https://www.acas.org.uk/early-conciliation). If the user thinks something was unfair, say "talk to Acas or a solicitor soon, because there are time limits" and add that as a plan line; don't calculate the limit for them.

**7. Documents to keep:** payslips (especially the last 12 weeks, which set the weekly pay figure), the redundancy letter, the settlement agreement, the P45 (it arrives after the last pay), pension statements. Same rule as the US file: only their own documents, never the employer's files.

## Other countries

Don't apply US or UK rules elsewhere. Find the government's official pages for the user's country on (1) unemployment or jobseeker benefits and how soon to claim, (2) notice and severance or redundancy pay, (3) health coverage after employment ends, if it's tied to the job, and (4) any release or settlement agreement rules. Put each as a plan line with the official link, or "check <government site>" when you can't confirm it. Many countries have a free government or union advice service for workers; point to it, and to a local employment lawyer for anything to sign.
