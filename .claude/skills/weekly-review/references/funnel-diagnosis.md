# Reading the funnel

Background for `/weekly-review` step 2. Researched 2026-10. Benchmarks vary hugely by field, level, country and market, so use them to spot the weakest stage, never to grade the user.

## Rough benchmarks (and how much to trust them)

- **Employer side:** across about 10 million applications to small US businesses in 2024, about 3 in 100 applicants got an interview and about 1 in 4 interviewees got an offer ([CareerPlug 2025 Recruiting Metrics Report](https://www.careerplug.com/recruiting-metrics-and-kpis/); vendor report from its own platform data). This counts everyone who clicked apply, including poor fits, so a tailored, good-fit search should beat it.
- **Job-seeker side:** tracker-app data (vendor-published, not peer-reviewed) shows response rates of a few percent for generic high-volume applying and noticeably higher for fewer, tailored applications. Direction is consistent across sources; the exact percentages are not.
- **Referrals:** across hiring studies, referred candidates are a small share of applicants and a much larger share of hires. Treat "a referral multiplies the odds" as well supported and any single multiplier as soft.
- **Ghost jobs:** Greenhouse estimated 18–22% of postings on its platform in a given quarter were not being actively hired for ([Greenhouse 2024 State of Job Hunting](https://www.greenhouse.com/blog/greenhouse-2024-state-of-job-hunting-report); vendor estimate). Some silence is about the posting, not the user.
- **Small numbers are noise.** Don't diagnose a stage with fewer than about 10 items in it. Say "too early to tell" instead.

## Stage by stage: what to change

| Where it stalls | Likely cause | Change next week |
|---|---|---|
| Few applications going out | Pace, or stuck tailoring | Two to three fixed search blocks on the calendar; `/find-jobs`; cap tailoring time per application |
| Applied, almost no responses (10–15+ applications) | Targeting or the resume's first look | Check fit scores of what they applied to (raise the bar); `/resume-review` on the top third; apply in the posting's first week; get a referral for A-priority jobs |
| Responses only from one source or role type | That's the signal | Shift time toward what's converting (by source, role type, resume version) |
| Recruiter screens, no next round | Pitch, salary answer, or a knockout (location, authorization, level) | Practice the 60–90 second "walk me through your background"; prepare the pay answer (`/offer` market range); check the roles match their level |
| Interviews, no final rounds | Stories and role-specific depth | `/mock-interview` on the weak areas from debriefs; fill story-bank gaps |
| Final rounds, no offers | Closing, or close competition | Questions they ask, thank-yous within 24 hours, reference prep; ask the recruiter politely for feedback |
| Silence everywhere after 3+ weeks | Market, season, or ghost postings | More networking conversations (warm leads convert best); check postings are still live before applying |

## Keeping it going

Search effort tends to fall over the months: in one study of 177 unemployed job seekers, weekly search time dropped from about 17 to 14 hours by month four, alongside a dip in wellbeing ([Wanberg et al., Georgia Tech summary](https://hg.gatech.edu/node/125891)). The same research points to fixed routines, visible progress and social support as what keeps people going. So put next week's plan on the calendar, keep it realistic, and count conversations and practice as progress, not just applications.
