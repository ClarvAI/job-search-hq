# Conversation guide

For `/network` step 5 and "After a conversation". Plain-English versions go to the user; the sources are here so the advice can be checked.

## Questions for an informational chat (15–20 minutes)

Pick about five, at least one from each group, tailored to the person's role. The order moves from easy to personal (career coach Steve Dalton's "TIARA" order: trends, insights, advice, resources, assignments).

- **Trends:** What's changing most in your area right now? What's the team spending more time on than a year ago?
- **Insights:** What surprised you most in your first year? What separates people who do well in this role?
- **Advice:** If you were in my position, what would you do in the next three months? What would make someone like me a stronger candidate?
- **Resources:** Is there a group, event, or publication people in your field actually use? Anyone else you think I should talk to?
- **Assignments:** What does a typical project for someone new look like? (For a good chat, it's fine to ask: "Would you be willing to look at my resume and tell me what you'd change?")

Always end with: "Who else should I talk to?" and "Is it OK if I keep you posted?"

Don't ask anything the company website answers, and don't ask for a job or referral in this call. If they offer one, say yes and thank them.

## The 30-second intro

Who the user is in one line, the one thing they're exploring, why this person. Example shape (fictional): "I'm Alex, three years in customer success at a software company. I'm looking at product marketing, and you made the same move, so I'd love to hear how you did it."

## Who replies, and pacing

- Expect most people not to reply. Dalton's rough rule: about 1 in 5 people are genuine helpers, and they're worth finding, so contact 2–3 people per company at once.
- His "3B7" routine: no reply in 3 business days → try a second person at the same company; at about 7 business days → one follow-up to the first person. This workspace sets the first person's follow-up date at 5 business days and stops after 2 touches.
- After a good chat: thank-you within 24 hours, then a short update when the user acts on their advice (about a month later is natural). People who see their advice used are the ones who refer.

## If the user dreads networking

- Writing first is fine: a short email or LinkedIn message, then a one-to-one call. One-to-one calls suit quieter people better than events.
- Bring written questions; it reads as prepared, not nervous.
- Aim for two or three real conversations a week, not volume.
- At events: arrive early, when rooms are small; pick 3–5 people to meet ahead of time; take a break between sessions.

## Career changers

- Talk to people who made the same switch. Ask what transferred, what they had to learn, and which job titles let them in.
- Use the conversations to learn the field's words, then use those words (only where true) in the resume and LinkedIn.
- A 2-line "bridge" pitch: what the user has done that matters in the new field, plus what they're doing to close the gap.

## Experienced hires

- Start with people they've worked with, including ones they haven't spoken to in years. A short, honest note ("it's been a while; I'm looking at X; would you have 15 minutes?") is normal.
- Ask former managers and peers about specific openings and introductions earlier than a student would; they can judge the user's work directly.
- Lead with a problem the user has solved that the other company likely has.
