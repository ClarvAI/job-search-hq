# start: adapt to their answers

Part of the `start` skill (how to ask, saving and progress tracking: its SKILL.md).

Setup is a conversation, not a form. Follow these, and use judgment for anything similar:

- **Already in hiring processes** (section 2): add each one to `jobs/tracker.csv` right away (status from what they say: applied / screening / interviewing / offer, with the real dates and a next step), create the job folder if there's a posting, and log it in `history/activity.md`. An interview within the next 7 days or an offer with a deadline comes first: say so, finish only the essentials (sections 1–3; section 3's save marks setup done and starts the field pack), and make the first-week plan start with `/interview-prep` or `/offer` for it.
- **A former employer mentioned hiring them (back)**: that's the strongest lead. Make the first item of the first-week plan a note to that person, and add them to `contacts/contacts.csv` (`relationship=former-manager`).
- **"Not sure" about roles** (question 7): give a two-line, plain description of 3–5 role types that fit their resume and suggest the two that match best; let them pick.
- **Career changer:** ask which parts of their past work they want to carry over, and which they'd rather leave behind. Target roles that bridge the two usually convert best; say so once.
- **Recently laid off or let go** (they say so, or `career_stage` is `returning` and the job ended in the last few weeks): say one kind sentence, then offer `/laid-off` before going on ("A few dates matter this week, like filing for unemployment and health coverage. Want me to put them in a short dated list first? It takes five minutes."). If the job ended within the last 30 days or they have a severance agreement or visa clock running, do it right after section 3's save, before the field pack talk. Don't ask why they left.
- **Claiming unemployment benefits:** offer `/work-search-log` once setup is done; it asks where they claim and keeps the weekly record.
- **On a visa that depends on having a job** (they mention OPT, an H-1B grace period, or similar): offer to fill the optional visa lines in `profile/preferences.md` (`visa_type`, `visa_clock_start` = the first counted unemployment day, `visa_days_allowed`, `visa_days_used` and `visa_days_used_as_of` = the date they read that count) so `/today` counts the days (formula: `.claude/skills/evaluate-job/references/visa-checks.md`, "Visa clock"); the numbers come from their DSO, the official counter or an immigration lawyer, never a guess, and never a visa or ID number.
- **Returning after a gap:** don't probe the reason. Ask only how they'd like it described ("one plain line" is the default) and save that under Notes. If they ask why it matters: some employers' application systems screen out unexplained gaps of six months or more (Harvard Business School, *Hidden Workers*, 2021), and one plain line on the resume handles it.
- **Outside the US:** work authorization, salary norms, resume conventions (photo, length, personal details, "CV" vs "resume") and job boards differ by country. Note the country in `home_base` (and set `paper: A4` in `profile/design.md`'s documents block where A4 is the norm), follow local norms in every skill (resume length and format by country: `.claude/skills/resume-review/references/what-recruiters-check.md`), and say once that some guidance (EEO questions, at-will employment, US-specific job boards) won't apply. Where Excel uses a comma for decimals (most of continental Europe and Latin America, among others), double-clicking a `.csv` file opens it all in one column, and saving it from Excel turns the commas into semicolons, which breaks the tracker. Tell them once, plainly: "To look at your tracker in Excel, use Data → From Text/CSV and pick comma, or open it in Google Sheets or Numbers. Please don't save it from Excel; just tell me what to change, or use the board."
- **Unsure about salary:** show the current market range for their top two role types and location, built per `.claude/skills/offer/references/pay-data.md` (sourced, dated, a range rather than one number), and save "not set; use market range" rather than pushing for a number.
- **Short answers, "this is a lot", or they want to stop:** compress. Finish sections 1–3 (the essentials; section 3's save marks setup done, so they aren't sent back to setup next time, and the field pack in section 4 still builds in the background), say the rest happens naturally later, and give the first-week plan.
- **They share something personal** (health, family, finances, immigration): note only what affects the search (for example "no travel before May"), never the details.
- **Long, detailed answers:** good. Keep them; they're material for stories and voice.
- **Resume in a connected file store** (Google Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive or Notion tools in the session; never ask them to connect one): offer "It's in my <store>" in question 1. Ask for the file's name, find it, read only that file (and only the portfolio pieces they pick), and handle it exactly like a file in `imports/` (sensitive-data check, copy rules). Never save, move or share anything there. Work account: `knowledge/data-connectors.md` rule 5.
- **Alumni step** (optional, after the target employers are listed; one question, skip it if they're compressing): "Want to see where people from <school in the master resume> work? It takes two minutes on LinkedIn, and I'll mark those employers as warm." On a yes, follow `.claude/skills/network/references/alumni.md` (a): they paste the school's Alumni page, filtered to their function and city; you note dated counts in `target-employers.md` and ask before adding any new company. Bootcamps and former employers count as a "school" too. Career changers: an occupation-data connector can help find bridge roles (`knowledge/data-connectors.md`).
