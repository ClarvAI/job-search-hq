# /interview-prep

Usage: `/interview-prep <job folder or company + role> [interview date/type]` (or just ask in plain English).

Job: `$ARGUMENTS`.

1. Read the job folder (posting, fit, resume, application answers), `profile/story-bank.md`, `knowledge/interview-questions.md` (question bank by competency and role), `knowledge/field-primer.md`, `knowledge/background.md`, and `references/formats-and-hard-questions.md` (what interviewers score, each format, hard questions, salary wording, sources). If a knowledge file still says `STATUS: not generated`, do a quick web lookup for what you need instead and offer to build it.
2. Ask the user (one message): date/time, format (recruiter phone screen, video, one-way recorded video, AI interviewer, onsite, panel, site or office tour, technical presentation, case or skills exercise), interviewer names/titles if known.
3. Run the `company-researcher` agent: what they make or do at this site or team, recent news (12 months), anything public a candidate in the user's field should know (e.g., a regulated industry may have public enforcement or recall records), competitors, culture signals, the team's likely work. Look up interviewers only through public, professional info (company pages, publications, talks). Don't browse LinkedIn for the user.
4. Write `jobs/<folder>/interview-prep.md`:
   - **Company in 60 seconds:** 5 bullets the user can say out loud.
   - **Why this company / why this role:** their real reasons, drafted from their preferences and fit; ask them to confirm.
   - **Walk me through your resume:** a 90-second version tuned to this role.
   - **Likely questions:** 8 behavioral (each mapped to a story-bank story; flag any with no good story) + 6–10 technical or role-specific ones from the field primer and the posting's stated requirements, picked by role type. Interviewers usually score the posting's skills on a rubric and then probe, so under each story note answers to: "What was your role?", "What was the result?", "What would you do differently?"
   - **Their likely weak spots** for this job (from fit.md gaps) and an honest way to answer each. If the resume shows a gap, layoff, firing or career change, draft a short answer from the user's own facts (wording in the reference, section 3) and have them say it out loud until it's easy.
   - **Questions to ask them:** 6 specific ones, matched to who they're meeting (recruiter: process and range; hiring manager: "How will you measure success in this role?", "What are the biggest challenges someone here will face?"; peers: a typical week). Always end with **"What are the next steps and the timeline?"** (the follow-up date depends on it).
   - **Recruiter phone screen:** a filter (must-haves, work authorization, location, start date, pay). Prepare the 90-second pitch, availability, and the salary answer.
   - **Salary question:** look for the pay range in the posting, and check whether the job's location requires employers to post ranges or bans asking about salary history (look up the current rules; they change). Where salary-history questions are banned, the user never has to share past pay. If a range is posted, don't ask for it; script: "The posted range of $X–$Y works for me; given <one real strength from the master resume> I'd hope to be in the upper half, but I'm looking at the whole package." If no range is posted: "I'd love to hear the range you have budgeted for this role first," and if pressed, give a researched range: the "Market pay" block in `fit.md` if it's recent, otherwise build one per `.claude/skills/offer/references/pay-data.md` and save it there. Base the user's number on that range and their floor in preferences, never a guess. Put it in the brief, with the sources so they can say where it comes from. If pressed, they give a range (bottom at or above their floor), never one number.
   - **One-way video (HireVue-style):** usually 3–6 questions, about 30 seconds to think and 1–3 minutes each, often one retake or none. HireVue says its scoring reads only a transcript of what's said, so a complete story with a clear result matters more than polish. Prepare "why this company / why this role" (plus "which rotations" for rotational programs) and 3 STAR stories under 2 minutes, check camera and lighting, and offer `/mock-interview <job> one-way`.
   - **AI interviewer (a bot by phone, video or chat):** treat it as a structured interview: answer each question fully in one turn, with an example and a number; speak clearly; never read AI-written answers. If the user lives in Illinois or New York City, or has a disability, tell them their notice and accommodation rights (reference, section 2). Offer `/mock-interview <job> ai-screen`.
   - **If the format includes a technical presentation:** pick one project, write a 7-slide outline timed to finish about a minute under the slot they gave (about 9 minutes for a 10-minute slot) (problem, context, approach, data, result, what I'd change, Q&A), scrub confidential material from past employers (no customer, client or product names, normalized charts; check with the former manager if unsure), list 6 likely probes with answers, and bring backups (USB, emailed copy, printed handouts). Offer `/mock-interview <job> presentation`.
   - **Site or office tour:** follow any safety or dress rules exactly (e.g., PPE on a factory floor or lab), don't touch equipment, stay with the host, ask one or two questions about how the work actually runs, and note one real observation to mention in the thank-you.
   - **References:** before a final-round or onsite interview, make sure 2–3 references are lined up in `profile/references.md` (former managers, or for students, co-op/internship supervisors or professors), with their permission, and offer to draft a heads-up note with the job description into `outbox/`.
   - **Integrity and ID checks:** many employers now verify identity on camera, and some forbid AI help during interviews and assessments. Prepare the user to do the live interview on their own, without AI, and to have photo ID ready if a verified employer asks. Never send ID numbers by email before an offer.
   - **Logistics:** time zone, link/address, what to wear (match the company and any tour rules), what to bring (printed resume, notebook, questions), 15-minute-early plan.
5. Run `truth-checker`, then `voice-editor`, on the first-person parts of `interview-prep.md` (the pitch, "why this company / role", weak-spot answers, presentation outline); re-check anything changed. Details still marked `[ASK]` stay out of answers until the user confirms them. Then offer a printable one-page brief (`/pages brief <folder>`) and flashcards for this job (`/pages flashcards`).
6. Update the tracker only if an employer has actually scheduled an interview (the user said so, or an email shows it): for a recruiter/HR phone or video screen or a one-way video screen, `status=screening`; for a hiring-manager, technical, panel or onsite interview, `status=interviewing`. Either way `next_action=Interview <date> (<type>)` and `next_action_date=<date>`, and log it in `history/activity.md`. If the user is just preparing ahead, leave the status alone. Offer `/mock-interview` with this job.
