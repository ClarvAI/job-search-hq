# Interview accommodations and disclosure

Background for `/interview-prep` and `/mock-interview`. Researched 2026-10. This is general information, not legal advice; laws differ by country and change. Labels: **Law** (statute or regulator), **Guidance** (official advice), **Common practice**.

## 1. The user decides

- **Whether to disclose a disability or health condition, to whom, and when is the user's choice.** Applicants don't have to disclose up front; the US EEOC says applicants decide when to tell an employer they need an accommodation, and that it's wise to ask as soon as they know they'll need one, since some take time to arrange (Guidance, EEOC).
- Asking for an interview accommodation doesn't require a diagnosis. The request can name only what would help ("extra time for the written exercise"). Under the ADA, applicants don't need to use words like "disability" or "ADA" to make a request (Guidance, JAN). An employer **may** ask for reasonable documentation when the need isn't obvious (Guidance, EEOC and JAN); the user can ask who will see it and send it only to HR, not the interviewers.
- **The assistant never raises the topic uninvited in a way that pressures disclosure,** never writes health details into a cover letter, resume or message unless the user asks, and never guesses at a condition.

## 2. Storing it

- Store only what the user chooses. **Never store a diagnosis or medical detail unless the user explicitly asks.** The accommodation itself ("needs questions in writing", "breaks every 45 minutes") can go in `profile/preferences.md` under "Notes from conversations" or in the job's `interview-prep.md`, only with the user's OK, worded as they choose. Offer to keep it out of files entirely.
- Never put it in `jobs/tracker.csv`, `contacts/contacts.csv` or `history/activity.md` (log "sent accommodation request to <company>" only if the user agrees; never the content).

## 3. Common interview accommodations

Examples from official guidance (EEOC, JAN, Acas) and common practice. The user picks what fits:
- Interview questions (or topics) in advance.
- Extra time for answers, tests or exercises.
- Questions in writing as well as spoken; a written-answer option.
- Breaks, or a shorter session split in two.
- Captioning on video calls, a sign language interpreter, a lip speaker.
- A quiet room, lighting or noise adjustments; an accessible building or room.
- Video instead of in person (or the reverse); a live human instead of an AI or one-way video interview.
- Accessible formats for documents and tests (large print, screen-reader-friendly files).
- A support person or communication support worker.

## 4. Draft: asking for an accommodation

Plain, short, no diagnosis needed. Draft into `outbox/` (draft only), addressed to the recruiter or the accommodation contact named in the invite (JAN recommends employers list one; check the email or careers page). Example shape, filled with real details:

> Subject: Accommodation request for my <date> interview
>
> Hi <Name>,
>
> Thank you for the invitation to interview for the <role> position on <date>. To take part on equal terms, I'm requesting an accommodation: <the specific adjustment, e.g. "the questions in writing at the start of each section" / "extra time for the written exercise" / "captions on the video call">.
>
> I'm happy to talk about what would work best. Could you let me know by <date a few days before> whether this can be arranged?
>
> Thank you,
> <First name>

Notes: send it early (as soon as the format is known). Keep the request to what's needed for the interview; the user can raise job accommodations later, after an offer, if they choose. If an employer asks for documentation, the user decides what to share. Run `voice-editor` on it; no `truth-checker` claims beyond the request itself.

## 5. Country notes

- **United States:** the ADA covers private employers with 15 or more employees and state and local governments, and applies to every part of hiring, including applications, tests and interviews (Law; EEOC). Employers may not ask disability-related questions before an offer, except to handle a requested accommodation (Guidance; JAN). Free, confidential help: the **Job Accommodation Network (askjan.org)**, run by the US Department of Labor, including sample request letters. Some states and cities add rules (e.g. notice and accommodation for automated interview tools in New York City; Illinois AI video interview rules); see `formats-and-hard-questions.md` §2.
- **United Kingdom:** under the Equality Act 2010, employers must make reasonable adjustments for disabled job applicants and interviewees; Acas guidance says employers shouldn't ask about health or disability during recruitment except to arrange adjustments (and a few other exceptions) and should ask interviewees whether they need adjustments (Law and Guidance; Acas). **Access to Work** can pay for communication support at a job interview (e.g. a BSL interpreter or lip speaker) for people in England, Scotland and Wales; costs are paid after the interview (Guidance; GOV.UK).
- **Elsewhere:** most countries have disability-equality law (e.g. EU member states under the Employment Equality Directive; Australia's Disability Discrimination Act with JobAccess advice). Check the local law or public employment service before stating a right; say "in many countries" rather than guessing.

## 6. Practising with an accommodation

Mock interviews can mirror it (see `/mock-interview` "Practising with an accommodation"): longer answer times, the question on screen with think time before the clock, reading aloud off, breaks between questions.
